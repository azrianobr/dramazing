The Last Tram

Every night at 11:40, the last tram leaves Harbour Street in the rain. Mae has been its conductor for thirty-one years.

Every night, the same man gets on at the third stop. Theo is in his late twenties, wears a grey raincoat, and never has a ticket. He sits by the rear window, rides to the end of the line, and walks back.

Tonight is Mae's last shift. She stops beside his seat, the ticket punch in her hand.

"Thirty-one years, and you are the only passenger I never charged. Tell me why you ride."

Theo keeps looking at the dark water. "My father drove this line. He said the last stop is where the city lets go of you. I never got to ride with him."

Mae is quiet for a moment. Then she punches a ticket and puts it in his hand. "Then this one's on him."

The tram turns the final corner. The harbour lights slide across the wet glass.
