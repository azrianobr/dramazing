# Prompt rules

Every rule here comes from testing. The parentheses say where a problem first appeared. The examples come from *Dukou (渡口)*, the sample work made with this workflow (see `examples/渡口/`).
The video tool tested was Grok. Wherever this page says "Grok will …", check again if you switch tools. The rules themselves (one action per shot, a clear end point, hold the first frame) apply to similar tools in general.
Most rules are already built into `scripts/video-prompts.mjs` and `scripts/targets/` and are added automatically when prompts are generated. The parts that need a human check are listed below too.

## Structure of a prompt

Every prompt that `video-prompts.mjs` generates follows this order (`grok` and `generic` have the same content and differ only in how the parts are joined):

1. Quality opening: film look; keep the first frame's composition, faces and costumes.
2. Shot header: ID | seconds | shot size | scene.
3. Camera setup: who it is aimed at, angle, lens.
4. Action and eyeline.
5. Camera move (if there is one).
6. Ambience.
7. Dialogue (the delivery described in English, the line itself in the story language).
8. Key constraints.
9. Number of people on screen.
10. Ending: no subtitles, no text, no music. In Grok prompts for shots with dialogue, this sentence moves to the very front; at the end, Grok still burns in subtitles (各论各的, 真的).

Everything is on one line, with no line breaks: pressing Enter in a web input box submits it immediately.

## Writing

- **Forbid actions directly; never name a forbidden object.** "Stays seated, never stands up" and "keeps their back to the camera the whole time, never turns around" work. "No bamboo hat" brings in a bamboo hat; write "this is the only person in the frame" instead.
- **Replace names with appearance phrases.** Grok does not know character names. Write an appearance phrase in `alias` in `project.json`.
- **Video tools ignore pronunciation notes; reword hard words.** Characters with more than one reading, or rare words, get read wrong. Writing "劫 is read jié" in the prompt does nothing: 「我是来抢劫的」 was still read jiè. Changing it to 「我是来打劫的」 was read right the first time (真的 E01-03/s1). Swap in a common word in the script; do not rely on pronunciation notes. Check this when writing dialogue for an adapted foreign-language film too: 「走到古城门得多久」 has 得, read dé or děi; it became 「去古城门要多久」 (走多快).
- **Write the order of line and action as "first …, then …", with the approximate second.** Given only an action and a line, the video tool says the line while doing the action, and the line often comes too early. For "walks to the door, then calls out", write "first walks to the door; at about second 3 the shopkeeper calls out" (真的 E01-01/s3 rework).
- **For a reaction beat, write it by the second: "look, freeze, speak, leave at once".** Written only as "glances over, smile freezes, forces out a line, turns away when done", Grok says the line first with no change of expression, then stands there for 3 seconds after it (拍张照 E01-02/s2, first take). Written as "0–1 s: looks down at it; 1–1.6 s: smile freezes, eyes widen; speaks only at 1.6 s; turns away right after the line and is out of frame before second 4", it was right the first time.
- **No dashes in dialogue.** A Chinese dash gets read out as 「一」 ("one"), so always use commas in dialogue and subtitles. For English and Korean, the generator replaces dashes with comma pauses.
- **A long line in a long shot: say it is spoken in one breath.** When a 10-second shot has a single long line with commas, the video model spreads it out and pauses a second or two at a comma; the "no long pauses" the generator adds does not stop it. In the action, write "says the whole line in one breath, no pause or breath at the commas, finished within the first N seconds", with N = characters ÷ 5 per second for Chinese (各论各的 E01-01/s1: 26 characters, N = 5; pause 1.9 s → 0.4 s, line done in 4.7 s).
- **Do not quote words of the line inside an action description.** With "when he says 「捡着了」, he puts the cash down", Grok drew those three characters into the picture (等同现金 01-2, second take). Write "after the first half of the line" or "after the line ends".
- **For a line of three characters or fewer, write "says only these words".** When a line is very short, Grok adds words of its own: "有有有！" became "欢迎光临……" (等同现金 01-6). In the limits, write "says only 「有有有」, these three characters, then closes the mouth".
- **Put delivery notes after the line, marked as not to be spoken.** In brackets before the line, they get read out as dialogue (in *Dukou* E03-11-1 the character said 「抹了把汗」, "wipes his sweat", aloud). In the script, `tone` is only the tone of voice; write actions as their own beat.
- **In the tone, do not write "slowly" or "drawn out"; write "speaks a bit fast".** With "says it slowly, dragging out the last syllable", Grok took 3.25 seconds for 10 characters and read one extra character; with "speaks a bit fast" it took 2.1 seconds, word for word (拍张照 E01-01/s5). Put the speech rate measured in a test shot into `speechRate` in `project.json`; otherwise the generator assumes 3 characters per second and makes dialogue shots 10 seconds long.
- **Do not write "word by word" for tone; for a demand, write "asks in one breath".** With "word by word", Grok pauses after each character, and the four-character "跟谁吃的？" became the slowest line in the film (2.5 characters per second); the user heard the pauses. "Asks in one breath, no pause between the four characters, holding back anger" took 0.7 seconds (跟谁吃的 E01-01/s5).
- **For a reaction shot, write "absolutely no smile, the corners of the mouth do not rise", and name the expression you want.** With only "stunned" or "speechless", Grok lets the emotion relax and the mouth turns up in the second half of the clip. For a reaction used as a cover, generate a separate clip with no dialogue: "only listens, says nothing, the frown deepens, the corners of the mouth turn down, no smile at any point" (跟谁吃的 02-3 cover: two crops from the end of 02-2 both had a smile; only a separate clip was clean).
- **After a meal or any event, write the state of every object.** With only "half a plate of food, a wine glass with a lipstick mark", the chopsticks, napkins and chopstick rests stayed perfectly neat, and the user saw it at once: "they have finished eating, why is it so tidy?" Write "both plates hold only sauce, the chopsticks lie across the plate rims, the napkins are crumpled on the table, the chopstick rests are empty", in the first frame and in the prompt (跟谁吃的 01-7, 02-4).
- **To make someone look somewhere, draw the gaze into the first frame; do not write a head-turn direction in the shot.** Grok ignored "turns back over his right shoulder to the door on the left of the frame, never to the right": two takes in a row turned to the right of the frame, 1.5–2 seconds late (那个女生 02-5, takes 3 and 4). With a first frame where he already stares at the door and the prompt "head and body still, staring at the bedroom door at the back left the whole time, only the expression changes", it worked in one take.
- **Put the source of an off-screen sound in the frame.** A child calls "Daddy" from behind the door: with a window behind him it sounded like the balcony; with the door outside the frame it sounded like it came through a wall (02-5, takes 1 and 2). With the closed bedroom door in the frame and his gaze on it, viewers hear the voice as coming from behind the door.
- **Do not name the source of an ambient sound in the prompt.** "The soft hum of the air conditioner" made Grok draw an air conditioner above the door frame (02-5, take 5). Leave ambient sound to post: delete it from the prompt and from the sound setting in `project.json`. Then handle it like anything else not in the first frame (see "Mention nothing in the prompt that is not in the first frame" in this section): "no air conditioner and no appliance on the wall, the background stays as in the first frame".
- **Hand business that prevents burned-in subtitles must look natural: hands off the face, hands off the clothes.** A single speaker needs continuous small actions, but a hand wiping her eyes the whole time was "very unnatural" to the user; gripping her pyjama trousers stretched the fabric and "makes the trousers look huge, not real" (那个女生 01-1, takes 1 and 2). Hands clasped on her lap, fingers twisting, thumb picking at the back of the other hand, with the first frame drawn in that pose, worked in one take. Other options: rubbing fingers, turning a ring, fiddling with a phone; for tears write only "wipes once quickly and lowers the hand".
- **Split contact actions such as a slap into two cuts: one on the person hit, one on the person hitting.** With one cut that has to land on the right cheek, turn the head the right way, cover the right side and also shout a line, Grok kept getting left and right wrong: two hands slapping, a right hand hitting the right cheek, hit on the left and covering the right, a palm sweeping past the chest without touching the face (道什么歉 01-2, takes 1–6). Showing only the swing out of frame, the user said "she just reached out, she never hit him" (take 7). What worked: a close-up of the person hit as its own cut, with a first frame where the hitter's hand comes in from off-screen, palm a few centimetres from his cheek at face height, and the prompt "keeps moving forward, never pulls back, hits once and swings through, his own hands stay down". It landed in one take and Grok added the slap sound itself; cut from where the hand starts moving and speed it up 1.7×, about 0.5 seconds. The next cut picks up the hitter's follow-through and then the line; fix which hand covers which cheek in the first frame of the cut after that.
- **No commas in short lines.** With "这孩子，不是你的" Grok paused almost 1 second at the comma, still 0.7 seconds after speed-up, and the user found it too long (道什么歉 01-1). Drop the comma from a line that can be said in one breath and write "in one breath, no pause" in the performance note; the re-take paused about 0.1 seconds. If you want a pause, write it into the action, not the punctuation.
- **For exaggerated physical acting, frame from the waist up and draw the exaggerated pose in the first frame.** In a chest-up close shot, "frightened" became only wide eyes and a hand on the chest, and the user wanted more (道什么歉 01-5, take 1). In a medium shot with a first frame of hunched shoulders, leaning away and both arms clutching the folder, plus a prompt for stepping back, head shaking and patting the chest, the action came through. Big movements often come after the line ends, so hold the cut longer with `fix.json` and do not let the automatic cut point land on the last word.
- **For the speaker, write "opens their mouth on every word".** With only "lips in sync", a tone such as "low voice" turns into a voiceover with a still mouth (E06-07-2).
- **Say whose voice an off-screen voice is.** With only "an off-screen reply", Grok borrows the voice of the person in the frame (光坐着 E01-02/s9: the off-screen boss's 「好嘞」 measured 178 Hz, the young man in the frame 175 Hz; it sounded like him). Write age, build, timbre and distance, and say it is completely different from the person in the frame: "a heavy-set boss in his fifties, far away in the back kitchen, hoarse voice, completely different from the young man's voice".
- **When someone off-screen speaks and the person in frame does not, leave the speaker out of the cut's `chars`.** When the speaker is not in `chars`, the generator writes the line as voice only (nobody in the frame moves their lips) instead of "mouth opens on every word". In 随叫随到 the off-screen wife has 7 lines, and the husband's mouth stayed shut in all 16 generations. Also write "he does not speak, his mouth stays shut" in `limits`.
- **Write a long-handled prop as one piece.** The mop broke into two pieces in the first version (随叫随到 s6). After writing "the mop handle is one straight aluminium pole, whole the entire time, never bending, breaking or splitting, the mop head always attached", both versions kept it whole. Do the same for brooms, umbrellas and fishing rods.
- **Time counting gestures in seconds, and draw the first frame at the starting count.** For "one piece of good news and one piece of bad news" she should raise one finger, then a second. The first frame showed two fingers, so Grok held two fingers the whole time, and the user pointed it out (《拖鞋》 s2). With a one-finger first frame, the action "raise one finger at the first piece of news" burned the line into subtitles in both videos. Rewritten in seconds, "raises her index finger at 0.2 s, raises her middle finger at 1.4 s", the gesture was right and the picture was clean. Referring to the line's content in the action burns subtitles just like quoting its words.
- **When someone points at a thing while talking to a person, the face goes to the listener, only the arm goes to the target, and the fingertip lands on the target.** In 《拖鞋》 s6 the doctor says the man in the next bed wants to buy his slippers. In the first version her fingertip pointed at a bare wall: the user said "she points at the wall". After a new first frame she looked at the curtain she was pointing at: the user said "she is not facing the patient". The first frame that worked shows her looking down at him, arm straight, index finger on the curtain fabric, with "looks at him the whole time, never turns to the curtain" in the prompt. The thing pointed at must be in frame.
- **The listener does not smile, and the speaker's tone carries no hope of a smile.** In s6 the doctor "smiles, waiting for him to be pleased", and Grok made him smile too; the user pointed it out. Delete those words and write the listener as "tears in his eyes, brows knitted, dazed, mouth closed, never smiles".
- **When you move a set element for composition, keep it clear of people.** To make the pointing clear I moved the curtain to his bedside, and it ended up touching his head and shoulders: "the curtain is stuck to the man" (s6 third version). The fix hangs it about half a metre outside the bed, with the bed rail and a strip of wall between them, and `limits` says "the curtain stays about half a metre from him and never touches his head or shoulders". After each change, recheck that the user's earlier notes still hold.
- **If a single-person close shot with a short line keeps burning subtitles, switch to an over-the-shoulder shot.** In 《岳父》 s2 and s4 only the son was in frame, each with a 2–4 character line; 5 of the first 6 videos burned subtitles, and hand actions or removing the Chinese word for subtitles did not help. No two-person cut in the same film burned any. With the other person's blurred head and shoulder at the right edge of the first frame, all 4 resubmitted videos were clean; of 8 later ones, 3 burned, far fewer than the close singles. The over-the-shoulder framing also shows the two people facing each other.
- **Put the reverse-shot camera behind the other person, and fix one look for the furniture between them.** In the first cut of 《岳父》 the son leaned on the counter in s2 and s4, but in s3 the owner seemed far away; the counter looked like a low table from one side and a two-tier bar from the other, with different things on top. The user pointed it out twice. s3 now shoots from behind the son, with his head and his forearm on the counter at the left edge, and all three cuts say "a waist-high single-tier light-wood counter with only one potted plant in a white pot", with the plant's position stated for both directions.
- **In a reverse shot, flip the distant people and the things in front of them.** Cut 1 shoots into the restaurant from the door: the mother faces the camera, the table is in front of her, the counter behind her. In s2 and s4, shot outward from the counter, she must be seen from behind with the table beyond her, farther from the camera. I first drew her face-on and the user said she later had her back to the counter; then I turned her around but left the table behind her on the camera side, and the user asked why the dishes were behind her. Flip every item for tiny background figures too: facing, the table in front, what is on it.
- **Grok cannot do spit-takes or spitting liquid; use a coughing fit first.** In 《岳父》 s5 the mother should spray her water on hearing the line. Four rounds failed: first a puff of vapor, then dribbling, then "a liquid jet" gave a hose with more water than she drank. A close shot with flat cheeks and "only one small sip" fixed the amount, but the user was still unhappy. "She suddenly chokes, bends over, covers her mouth and coughs hard" passed first time, and the laugh still lands on her reaction. Before writing a spit-take, ask whether choking, freezing or dropped chopsticks will do.
- **When the original audio is kept and Grok only lip-syncs, still write the real line and "says the line aloud".** 《预制的》 uses the source video's male and female voices for the whole episode (episode-level `mute: true`). The first round wrote only "mouth moving, no sound" with no line text, and the mouth shapes did not match the audio; the user pointed it out. With the real line plus "says the line aloud", transcribe each clip's speech, measure when the first line starts, and pick clips within 0.15 s of the original. Check every line in multi-line cuts: the first picks for s4 and s10 were 0.4–0.7 s off on the later lines, and each took another round. The cost is that about half the clips burn subtitles, so detect them automatically and resubmit. The detector misses small white text without a dark outline (one s13 clip had 「你女儿」 at the bottom), so look at the frames too when picking.
- **Keep nothing in hands or bags that the story does not use.** 《预制的》 is a chance meeting on the street. In the first cut leeks stuck out of the mother's canvas tote; in the second she carried a durian in a red net bag. The vegetables and the net bag changed shape and position from cut to cut, and the user said twice that they "give it away". Delete props that do not drive the story: the sheet and first frames say "right hand empty, hanging naturally; tote closed, contents not visible", and `limits` says "she holds nothing". Cover an object named in a line (「我现在种榴莲」) with a gesture, such as pointing at her own chest.
- **When two people talk about an off-screen child, keep their eyes on each other.** In 《预制的》 s12–s16 they talk about the daughter, and both first frames had them looking down. The user said "while talking about the daughter, their eyes are on the durian." The child is not in frame, so a downward gaze reads as looking at something in the hand or on the ground. Redraw the first frames with them looking up at each other, write "keeps looking into the other's eyes, does not look down", and add "does not look at their hands or the ground" to `limits`. Only the cut where she speaks directly to the child (s19 「听话咱不吃」) looks down below the frame, with her hand reaching past the bottom edge as if holding the child's hand.
- **For a silent listening cut, pick a window where the mouth stays closed, and slow it down if it is too short.** In 《预制的》 s12 the mother hears her off-screen daughter call 「妈咪」, and in the clip she moves her mouth for the first 3 s. Set `in` to 3.0 s in `fix.json` and `speed` to 0.88 to fill the length; 12% slower is not noticeable.
- **For a 10-second cut, choose 10 s by hand on the Grok page.** The page defaults to 6 s and does not take the storyboard duration, so long lines get cut off. Switch the duration menu to 10 s before submitting.
- **If the story needs a lamp to stay off, take the light-emitting part out in the first frame.** In 别教我 a repairman fixes a desk lamp in the living room. The lamp was off and unplugged in the first frame, and the prompt said "off the whole time, not lit", yet Grok lit it as soon as he turned a screw or held the lamp head: 5 clips for s1 and s6 lit up. In one of them the bulb glowed white; I only checked the inside of the shade and missed it, and the user asked "why did the lamp suddenly come on when he says 师父". After f6 was changed so the bulb lies unscrewed on the table and the shade opening faces away, both clips stayed dark. When checking, look at the bulb itself, not only the shade.
- **Put the ceiling light or the ceiling into the top of the first frame, and scan the top brightness when picking.** When the first frame stops at the wall with no light above, Grok tilts up slightly and brings in a lit ceiling light out of nowhere, which reads as the light switching on. It happened in s3, s6 and s8 of 别教我; I was watching the desk lamp, not the ceiling, and the user pointed it out a second time ("why did the light overhead suddenly come on"). Fixes: move `in` for s8 past the moment the light appears; crop the top 10%–16% of s3 and s6. When picking, count the bright pixels at the top of each frame; if the count jumps inside a shot, pick another clip or crop.
- **Fill the background of a single-person close shot; do not leave a bare wall.** In 别教我 the first frames of s3 and s7 had only a wall and a door behind the son, and Grok added a window, a pendant light, an old TV and a small lit lamp by itself; "keep the background exactly as in the first frame" did not help. s7 was saved by cropping 26% off the left to remove the lamp. Draw what the room should contain in the first frame, so Grok has no gap to fill.
- **When picking, check that the speaker's mouth moves; the transcript is not enough.** The first pick for s8 of 别教我 transcribed word for word, but the father kept his mouth shut, so the line played as a voice-over and the only person in frame who could be saying it was the repairman in the foreground; the user asked "why is the repairman saying it". The prompt did say the father speaks. Pull frames from the speaking part and confirm the speaker's lips follow the line.
- **After submitting to Grok, wait on the post page for the real address before leaving; send credentials when downloading.** Leaving within 5 seconds of submitting often leaves the post empty: in the first round of 别教我, 3 of 8 submissions produced nothing. Waiting 15–25 seconds until the address changes from `tmp_` to a real id almost stopped the empty posts. Downloads from assets.grok.com need `fetch(url, {credentials: 'include'})`; otherwise they return 403 and a 0-byte file.
- **When people sit facing water or an object and talk, put every camera on the side behind them.** In 干拌吗 the couple fish by a river. The first dialogue shots looked back from the water: faces to camera, river behind their heads, and the user said "you can tell they have their backs to the pond". All cameras moved to the bank behind them, with the river in the top half of the frame, in front of them. The over-shoulder shots say "body and knees face the river, only the head and shoulders turn", with the rod in front of the knees reaching into the water.
- **For light outdoor small-talk comedy, start with a bright season and light clothes.** The first 干拌吗 was autumn, overcast, dry grass and reeds, coats and padded jackets; the user said "too desolate". Summer sun, green trees and grass, short sleeves and shorts got "very satisfied, especially the character looks". A season change means remaking the character sheets too, then checking the clothes in every first frame.
- **When Grok misreads a word, write a homophone in the prompt.** In 干拌吗 s3 both takes said 手引 for 蚯蚓 (confirmed by the user). Writing 秋引 got it right; 蚯蚓 plus a pinyin note was still wrong and burned subtitles. Subtitles come from our SRT, so the prompt text only drives pronunciation. If the voice must change as well, dub with MiMo instead (`workflow.md` step 10).
- **Split long subtitle lines in the script.** A line over about 8 characters wraps to two lines or fills one line. At the 干拌吗 final cut the user asked for segments: split it into several `say` entries in `script.json` at the dub's pauses (我发现钓鱼人的饵料 / 都是河边现拌的), update the storyboard `beats`, then retime the subtitles to the pauses.
- **Layout notes describe only what this cut's frame shows.** In 改口费 s7, Xiaolin stands by the door, and the prompt kept the whole-room layout "the right wall is a rack with two white garment bags". The cut never shows the right wall, so Grok invented a hanging white dress when the camera drifted; the user said "a piece of clothing is floating by the mirror". Write only what is in the frame (the door, the dressing table, the mirror) and add "the wall next to the mirror is empty, no hanging clothes". The regenerated take kept the background still.
- **For a cut with a very short line, write the seconds after the line as small concrete actions.** 改口费 s9 is only 「跟我」 (two syllables). In a 6-second take Grok cut to a wide shot and dissolved one second after the line, and burned in a subtitle. Adding "after the line it stays the same close-up and she keeps looking at her for five seconds: one blink, chin slightly up, no cut to a wide shot, no dissolve, no angle change" gave a take with no cut on the second try.
- **Do not stage a line as "reading off paper".** In 账本 s3 the sister looks down and reads a list from the notebook ("2008, spicy strips, fifty fen"). In the first 4 rounds, 6 of 7 takes burned in subtitles; removing the Chinese word for subtitles, spoken-style years, and "say" instead of "read" all failed. Only a structural change worked: redraw the first frame so she looks up at her brother, merge the lines into two, drop the acting notes. Round 5 was clean. At storyboard time, turn reading into talking to the other person; if that fails, split into short cuts.
- **Close-up prompts leave out clothes and shoes outside the frame.** 账本 s3 is a chest-up close-up, but the character description kept "dark red velvet slippers", and Grok put the slippers on her lap. "Describe only what is in the frame" covers costume too: if a cut cannot see the feet, do not write shoes.
- **Do not write "northern accent" in the voice description; rewrite lines that end on an erhua-prone word.** In 账本 s3 the line ended in 「一块」 with a northern accent, and Grok said 「一块儿」; the user flagged it twice. "Standard Mandarin, no erhua" still came out as 「一块儿」 to the user's ear. Changing the line to 「一块钱」 worked first time. When a line ends on 块, 点, 会 or 门, change the line first.
- **Use a homophone for polyphonic characters too.** In 账本 s8 「我啥时候还过」, Grok read 还 (huán) as hái. The prompt line became 「我啥时候环过」 while the subtitle kept 还; 3 of 4 takes read it right. Speech-to-text cannot tell the readings apart, so the user has to listen.
- **A form of address followed by a long line tends to burn subtitles; cut the address out separately.** In 账本 s11 「哥，你在工地一个月才挣几个钱」 the first take dropped 「哥」; all 4 retakes said it but all burned subtitles. We cut 「哥」 from one retake, laid it over the end of the previous cut as off-screen voice, and kept the clean take for s11.
- **Close-up first frames must decide the ceiling.** The 账本 wide shot has one bare bulb. The brother's four close-up first frames did not show the ceiling, so Grok added a shaded pendant lamp at the top that did not match. The only fix left was cropping the top 12% in the edit. Either draw the same bulb into the close-up first frame or set the camera low enough that no ceiling shows.
- **In inner-voice shots, the speaker faces the camera with their mouth closed.** If someone else is in the frame, or the speaker has their back to the camera, it looks like they are talking to someone.
- **For a snub, keep elbows tucked and the prop against the chest.** A hand holding something held out from the body reads as offering it to the other person: the user said her hand reached out so far "it looks like she's handing him the seeds" (走多快 s2, second take). Write a sidelong glance, a sip of tea, then a lift of the chin as she turns away, with "the glass stays against her chest, elbows close to her body". This cut has no dialogue; keep it over 3 seconds in `fix.json` so the cold silence lands.
- **After a prop leaves the hand, say the hand is empty and there is only one.** "There is only one fan in the whole shot; after she throws it, both her hands are empty." Without this, Grok draws another fan in her hand (狐月 x1, first take).
- **State the physical state of small props.** "Loose, rolling at the bottom of the paper bag, none falling out". Otherwise they look glued to the hand (E02, the roasted beans).
- **Mention nothing in the prompt that is not in the first frame, scene name included.** The first frame had no bench, but the scene name the generator used was "a bench by a tree-lined path in the park", and Grok drew one in front of the character (老地方 E01-01/s8); writing "the phone rings" put a phone on the seat (E01-03/s3). For that cut, `place` replaced the scene name and the prompt added "no chairs of any kind in the frame"; it came out clean the first time. When the scene name or another sentence has already brought something in, forbidding it by name helps; anything nobody has mentioned still follows the first rule in this section (never name a forbidden object): do not name it.
- **Pin down hand props across cuts.** Which hand holds what, where it goes once put down, what the other hand holds: write it the same in both cuts, and add "nothing on the seat". When this is incomplete, the phone disappears, turns into a clutch bag, or a second one appears on the seat (老地方 E01-03/s6, five takes).
- **For running and walking, write "normal speed, not slow motion".**
- **For period objects, state the period and the material.** "An old wooden boat with an awning"; otherwise you get a speedboat.
- **Write constraints that the storyboard cannot imply into the cut's `limits` by hand.**
- **"No cuts" everywhere.** Every prompt includes "one continuous shot with no cuts, no jumps, no change of camera position". "One take" alone can still change the camera position midway.

## Characters

- **Put unusual features in `trait`.** Grok will "fix" features such as a blind eye or a scar; a correct first frame cannot hold them. When the character faces the camera, the generator writes "keep it throughout" once in English and once in the story language. When the character has their back to the camera, it leaves this out, so it does not make them turn around (E05-03-1, 06-1).
- **State that a young woman is an adult.** A frightened expression, freckles or an upturned face make a character look childlike. Grok then judges that "the image shows a minor" and produces no video, without any error. In both the sheet and the first frame, write "an adult woman in her early twenties, with a restrained expression" (E04-14-2).
- **No names in hand / prop close-ups.** With a name, Grok draws the whole person, sometimes two of them. The generator writes "only hands, sleeves and props in the frame; the owner of the hands stays off-screen" (E06-01-1).
- **When the eyeline target is off-screen, give only the direction.** Write "looks off-screen, frame right", not who that person is. With "the young woman off-screen", Grok draws her into the frame during a push-in (E05-03-1 gained an extra little girl).
- **Faces in wide shots get redrawn.** Do not use a wide shot when someone speaks; make the first frame a medium shot or a medium close-up. The same holds for the first frame of a push-in: when the person fills only a fifth of the frame, Grok redraws the face during the push (狐月 vertical f1). Make the person about two thirds of the frame height, and let the face leave the frame during the push instead of growing.
- **When two people sit apart, a single shot must not show the other one in the foreground.** If the first frame shows even a corner of the other person's table, half a shoulder or a few strands of hair, Grok pulls the whole person and table into the frame, and they look as if they sit together (光坐着 E01-02/s1, s3, s6, s7: four times). Draw the other side of the first frame as an empty aisle, and write in the limits "the aisle on the other side stays empty the whole time, no second person".
- **Do not write emotions as physical reactions.** "Face flushed" or "red-faced" becomes two red blotches on the cheeks, and retakes do not remove them (光坐着 E01-03/s1, twice). Write what the brows, eyes and mouth do: frowns, glares, bites her lower lip; then add "natural skin tone, no blush".
- **If a shot has someone turn to show their face, do not use a back view as the first frame.** A back view has no face and no front of the clothes, so when the person turns, Grok makes them up: the face is not hers, and the front of the wrap dress became a cowl neck (光坐着 E01-03/s1). Draw the first frame side-on, showing the profile and the front of the clothes.
- **Do not write "spins" or "turns the body around".** When a person turns half a circle in the air, Grok draws the far side as a front too, so the body has two fronts (狐月 s13). Write "the body keeps the same facing throughout; only the arms swing". To show rotation, let a prop or the sleeves turn, not the body.
- **Attach light effects to props, not to bodies or tails.** A light trail swung from a tail was drawn as a second tail (狐月 vertical s6); a light arc swung from a fan came out as light. For fire on a tail, write only "a small tuft of flame at the tail tip, no light trail".
- **Describe an unusual eye by its exact shape, and attach a reference of the eye.** "Slit pupils" alone gave round pupils (狐月 vertical s3). Write "the pupil is a thin vertical black slit, like a cat's or a fox's, not a round pupil", and attach the eye close-up cropped from the character sheet. It came out right the first time.
- **Write the simplest sitting pose.** Poses such as crossed legs or both hands on the thighs get drawn with an extra hand or with the left and right feet swapped, and the video acts it out (老地方 E01-01/s7 had three hands; the user only noticed after the final cut). Write "both hands folded in her lap, legs together, both feet side by side on the ground", and add "she has only two hands".
- **Keep other people's hands out of single shots.** When someone off-screen reaches into the frame, left and right hands are easily swapped, and viewers cannot tell whose hand it is (老地方 E01-02/s3: changed twice, then dropped). To show the action, put both people in the same frame.
- **In a first frame with two people, put them side by side with space between them.** When one stands behind the other, mostly hidden, Grok makes one of them vanish (等同现金 01-4, twice: once the assistant, once the owner). With the two side by side and background showing between them, the third take worked; 02-5, with the same composition, was changed too and worked first time.
- **Do not put a speaking character in a three-person wide shot.** In a three-person wide shot each face is a small part of the frame; when one of them speaks, Grok redraws that face, and the positions get rearranged by the action (等同现金 01-2: his face changed and the assistant moved from behind the counter to in front of it). Shoot the speaking beat as a single medium close-up; leave the spatial layout to a shared shot where nobody speaks.
- **When two faces come close, say they do not kiss.** For a selfie, "the two faces pressed together" made Grok have them kiss (拍张照 E01-02/s7, cut around in the edit). Write "cheeks close but not touching, no kiss".

## Camera moves

### First ask whether to move

For shots with dialogue or clear acting, default to a static shot (`Static Shot`): the audience can see the performance clearly only when the camera stays still. A camera move needs a narrative reason:

| What you want | Use |
|---|---|
| Get closer to the character's mind | `Push In` |
| Establish the setting, show isolation, close a scene | `Pull Out` |
| Reveal something off-screen | `Pan`, `Tilt`, `Rack Focus` |
| Walk along with a character | `Tracking Shot` |
| Conflict, panic, staying close to a character | `Handheld` |
| Rise to a wide view at the opening, rise and pull away at the end | `Crane` |
| See through a character's eyes | `POV` |

Do not move the camera just to look sophisticated.

**A film with only one location gets its variety from camera moves.** One move per cut, and each move serves one dramatic purpose. 走多快 is one street and one doorway: the opening cranes down from the eaves to eye level to show the old street and the gate; she ignores him on a static shot; the once-over is her POV, tilting from his face down to his shoes, setting up the closing line "I had to see how fast you walk"; as he leaves, a crane up shows how far the gate is; the shout gets a fast push-in; his shout back is an over-the-shoulder rack focus; the ending is a slow push-in. All 7 moves worked on the first take.

### One camera move per shot, with start, end and stop point

- **One main camera move per shot.** A push-in, then a pan, then an orbit easily breaks a video only a few seconds long. Split complex staging into two shots.
- **Write it all: name, direction, speed, subject, start and end framing, stop point.** With only "push in", the AI keeps pushing until the shot ends. Write it in the cut's `move`, and the generator turns it into "from … to …, stopping when …":

```json
"move": { "from": "a waist-up medium shot", "to": "a close-up of the face", "stop": "she looks up", "dir": "right", "speed": "slowly", "target": "陆行远" }
```

- **Draw the first frame as the start of the move.** Grok moves from the first frame toward the end point; if the first frame already has the end composition, there is nowhere to move. For a pull-out, the first frame is the close shot where the move starts; for a pan, the target is still out of frame; for a rack focus, only the starting layer is sharp. Of the 13 camera moves in *Dukou* E06, 4 failed, all for this reason.
- **The push-in is the most reliable.** It works best with an emotionally heavy line, stopping as the line ends.
- **A push-in on a shot/reverse-shot dialogue cut paints background that is not in the first frame.** In the first round of 《拖鞋》, s2 and s4 pushed in and a curtain appeared behind her; s5 grew blinds and another bed behind him. Use a static camera for dialogue shot/reverse shots, and write in `limits` exactly what is behind each person: "behind her only the window, blinds and bedside cabinet, no curtain". Keep camera moves for two-shots and silent reaction shots.

### Three pairs of moves that are easy to confuse

| This | Is not | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Pan | Truck | A pan rotates in place with the camera position fixed; a truck moves the whole camera sideways |
| Tilt | Pedestal | A tilt looks up or down in place; a pedestal raises or lowers the whole camera |
| Push / pull | Zoom | In a push or pull the camera really moves forward or back, and the background shows parallax; we always write "focal length unchanged" |

### Keep the actor and the camera from colliding

When a character walks toward the camera, state whether the camera waits in place or moves back to keep its distance. For a tracking shot, set `move.dir`: behind / front / left / right. "front" means the camera moves backward while the character walks toward it; the constraint then changes automatically to "the character never walks into the lens".

### Handheld, POV, two phases

- **Handheld is a texture, not a path.** Set `move.level` to restrained / documentary / intense (default: restrained), then say whom the camera follows, from which direction and how far away.
- **Point of view (POV).** Set `move.who` to whose eyes these are. Only that person's hands and sleeves may appear in the frame, and they cannot speak, because the audience cannot see their mouth.
- **Two phases.** `move.then = { camera, trigger, ... }` is written as "Phase one: …. When [trigger], phase two: …". Use it only for 10-second shots. The second move must have a trigger.

**One take** (several people introduced one by one, ending on a wide shot) goes beyond one camera move per shot. It has its own method; see `one-take.md`. Key points: make the camera the subject, not the people. Do not write turns or footsteps.

**Action showcase** (no plot, the subject keeps moving, shots joined by occluders, transformations done inside keyframe shots): see `showcase.md`.

**Untested camera moves** (two phases): the first time you use one, make a trial shot of that shot, and generate the batch only after it passes. The generator's preflight reminds you. Crane and POV have passed on Grok.

### Moves we do not use

- Drone / FPV: needs a route to fly through, so it does not work indoors or in tight spaces. It also breaks the mood of a period drama.
- Camera roll: too showy for restrained storytelling.
- Dolly zoom (Hitchcock zoom): hard for AI, and it needs a depth reference such as a corridor. 夜馆 tried to fake it by enlarging pull-out footage frame by frame. The user's verdict: 「整体看就不协调」 ("looks off as a whole").
- Dutch angle: a static tilted composition, not a camera move. The image service cannot draw a tilt. Rotate the first frame with a script after drawing, then enlarge it to crop off the black corners (夜馆).
